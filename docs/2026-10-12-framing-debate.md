# Why We Do Physics: phrasing for tomorrow

Prepared October 11, 2026, for review October 12.

Status: proposals only. Sabrina requested an agent debate and a saved Markdown note. No site copy, form, backend, or deployment was changed. Nothing below is approved for application.

## What Sabrina wants

- A global portrait of the physics community through people's own reasons for doing physics.
- See common themes and how they differ across locations, career stages, and subfields, while celebrating researchers.
- Reach beyond hep-th as her networks permit.
- Natural, concise wording. Avoid corny encouragement, promotional language, and institutional mission prose.
- A clear submission invitation. Describe what would be helpful rather than telling people what to do; put sharing after the submission logistics.
- No first-person "I" in general invitation copy.
- Theoria, PaperView, and traveling were background about what recipients already knew, not instructions to insert those topics into the email.

## What prompted this review

Robin's friend asked whether the site was a collection of testimonies, whether it was meant as motivation, and whether there was more to it. Robin connected it to scientists' values and agency as AI enters research. Sabrina welcomed that reading and described a global portrait built around a simple question.

Adam asked what the goal was and whether there was a planned venue. Sabrina clarified the themes/locations/stages/subfields goal above. A particular venue or publication plan has not been established.

These exchanges suggest that the question and submission mechanism are understandable, but the purpose of collecting the answers could be clearer. They do not establish why other people have or have not submitted.

Existing About text at the time of review:

> Physics is changing. The reasons why we do it will shape what it becomes. This project records those reasons, one short declaration at a time. Add yours.

## Agent debate

Three agents reviewed independently, then received competing positions and a shared candidate for cross-examination.

| Agent | Position | Criticism and resulting judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bohr | Put the portrait and celebration first; explain AI separately. | "Global portrait" becomes more informative when it names the differences Sabrina wants to explore. "How they vary" sounds more like an analysis plan than "what they have in common and how they differ." Wanted the AI context to retain researchers' agency. |
| Einstein | Explain why the collection matters, including values and the field's future. | Celebration alone leaves the purpose vague. Supported naming locations, career stages, and subfields. Proposed a stronger optional sentence connecting researchers' reasons to decisions about AI's role. |
| Peirce | Make the purpose concrete without implying a formal survey. | Initially warned that comparison categories could sound like a study. In the second round, accepted them when framed as an aim rather than a promised finding. Objected to turning Robin's interpretation into a new project mandate. |

Actual disagreement: how strongly to connect the portrait to shaping AI. Bohr and Einstein favored a more explicit values-and-agency sentence; Peirce favored preserving that as context until Sabrina decides whether it is an explicit project goal.

Synthesis: the portrait is the primary purpose; AI explains part of the origin and motivation. Keep the cross-community comparisons because Sabrina explicitly wants them. Describe an exploratory aim, without promising representative findings, policy influence, or an eventual declaration.

All three agents suggested making the contribution primary and sharing secondary. Some proposed "please share" wording in the debate; that wording was rejected here because Sabrina specifically prefers describing what would help.

## Recommended About candidate

> Why do you do physics? This project brings together short answers from across the field to build a global portrait of the community and celebrate the people doing the work. The aim is to see what people's reasons have in common and how they differ across places, career stages, and subfields.

Why this candidate: it answers what the collection is for, retains the celebratory purpose, and makes participation relevant across physics. It keeps the original question broad rather than turning the form into a question about AI.

## Optional context paragraph

> The project grew out of discussions at Perimeter Institute about meaningful research in the age of AI. Understanding what people value about doing physics can help frame discussions about how the field is changing.

This explains the origin without assuming the reader knows PI. It could sit separately from the short portrait description if Sabrina chooses to include it.

Stronger alternative for the second sentence, still undecided:

> The reasons people do physics matter when deciding how AI should fit into the work.

This better retains Robin's agency point, but makes AI more central. Decide tomorrow whether that is the intended emphasis. It should not imply that submitting an answer directly determines policy or technology decisions.

## Contribution and sharing wording

Keep the concrete logistics: responses are up to 140 characters and appear publicly with name and affiliation.

For personal or forwardable email, the current wording is already clear:

> It would be great if you could add yours. You can submit directly on the site—up to 140 characters, displayed with your name and affiliation.
>
> Sharing it with friends and colleagues who might like to contribute would also be very helpful.

If a more specific reason for sharing is wanted:

> Sharing it with friends and colleagues in other areas of physics would also help broaden the portrait.

The latter supports outreach beyond the current network, but may be too narrow for a general invitation. Keep it as an option, not an automatic replacement.

## Things to avoid

- Claiming a representative survey, statistical conclusions, or systematic analysis that has not been planned.
- Promising a venue, publication, institutional measures, or declaration.
- Presenting the collection primarily as a survey of attitudes toward AI.
- Assuming recipients know about PI or the recent declarations.
- Replacing the question with "what keeps you doing physics," which introduces a persistence/retention framing.
- Stacking descriptions such as "celebratory, bottom-up contribution to the conversation" without explaining the actual purpose.
- Adding simultaneous requests for submission, feedback, advice, and sharing to the main invitation.
